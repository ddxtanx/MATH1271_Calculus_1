\section{Challenge Problem}
In class we used the chain rule to 
calculate the derivative of a complicated composite function
in terms of its simpler component functions.
We can also use the chain rule to work ``the other direction"
i.e. if we have two complicated functions whose composite
is simple.

For example, take the natural logarithm function $\ln(x)$.
It is defined to be the \textit{inverse} function to $e^x$,
so it's domain is $(0, \infty)$ and it satisfies the identities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $e^{\ln(x)} = x$ for all $x > 0$
  \item $\ln(e^x) = x$ for all real $x$.
\end{itemize}

Apply chain rule to the above identities to determine the derivative of $\ln(x)$.
