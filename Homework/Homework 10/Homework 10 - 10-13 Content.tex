\section{Type 3 - Challenge Problem}
The product rule
\begin{equation}
  \dfrac{d}{dx}(f(x)g(x)) = (\dfrac{d}{dx} f(x)) \cdot g(x) + f(x) \cdot (\dfrac{d}{dx} g(x))
\end{equation}
is so important that it gets \textit{another} name - it's alternatively called the Leibniz Rule.
Demonstrate why the product rule is so important by proving
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The product rule forces $\dfrac{d}{dx} (1) = 0$. (Think of $\dfrac{d}{dx} 1^2$)
  \item The product rule forces the constant multiple rule $\dfrac{d}{dx} (c \cdot f(x)) = c \dfrac{d}{dx} (f(x))$.
  \item Together with $\dfrac{d}{dx} x = 1$, the product rule forces the power rule $\dfrac{d}{dx} x^n = n x^{n - 1}$. (Hint $x^n = x \cdot x^{n - 1}$ for $n \geq 1$).
\end{enumerate}

I.e. for each of the above goals, use only the product rule (plus the additional assumption for the third one) to prove that the forced rule must hold.
