  \question[5] Compute the limit
  \begin{equation}
    \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{\ln^2(x)}{x}
  \end{equation}
  using L'Hôpital's rule.

  \begin{solution}
    We first need to determine if the limit is in a form where L'Hôpital's rule applies.
    As $x \rightarrow \infty$, we know that $\ln(x) \rightarrow \infty$ too,
    thus this limit is an indeterminate form of type $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$,
    and thus we can apply L'Hôpital's rule.

    Since $\dfrac{d}{dx} \ln^2(x) = \left(\dfrac{d}{du}\mid_{u = \ln(x)} u^2 \right) \cdot \left(\dfrac{d}{dx} \ln(x)\right) = 2 \ln(x) \cdot \frac{1}{x}$
    and $\dfrac{d}{dx} x = 1$ we have that
    \begin{align}
      \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{\ln^2(x)}{x} &= \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{\frac{d}{dx} \ln^2(x)}{\frac{d}{dx} x} \\
                                                             &= \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{2 \ln(x) \cdot \frac{1}{x}}{1}.
    \end{align}

    When we try and analyze this limit, we note that it's current form is hard to interpret and so we simplify the fraction to get
    \begin{equation}
      \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{2\ln(x) \cdot \frac{1}{x}}{1} = \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{2\ln(x)}{x}
    \end{equation}
    which, by the above reasoning, is indeterminate of type $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$.

    We again apply L'Hôpital's rule to get:
    \begin{align}
      \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{2\ln(x)}{x} &= \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{\frac{d}{dx} 2 \ln(x)}{\frac{d}{dx} x} \\
                                                            &= \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{2 \cdot \frac{1}{x}}{1} \\
                                                            &= \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{2}{x} = 0.
    \end{align}

    Therefore, we have that:
    \begin{equation}
      \lim\limits_{x \rightarrow \infty} \dfrac{\ln^2(x)}{x} = 0.
    \end{equation}
  \end{solution}
